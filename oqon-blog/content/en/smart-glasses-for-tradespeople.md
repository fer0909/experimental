## The problem: good work nobody can prove

Mechanics, electricians, plumbers, carpenters and welders share one problem. The finished result is visible, but what was there before, and what ended up behind the wall or under the hood, is not. Weeks later comes the "that was already broken," the "you never did that," or the question of why the job cost what it cost.

The known fix is to take photos. The obstacle is practical: your hands are greasy, dusty or gloved, you're up a ladder or under a vehicle, and pulling out a phone, unlocking it and aiming costs time. So the photo gets taken "at the end," when it no longer proves much.

## What to record on every job

Job-site documentation guides agree on a simple order. According to [a guide on what to photograph on a job site](https://companycam.com/resources/blog/what-to-photograph-on-a-job-site-to-protect-against-liability), you should:

1. **Photograph the starting condition** before work begins, to have a baseline against claims of pre-existing damage.
2. **Document what will be hidden** (connections, pipe runs, wiring, joints) before it is covered, with a wide shot and a close-up, plus a scale reference such as a tape measure.
3. **Repeat the same positions** as work progresses to build a comparable timeline.
4. **Photograph payment milestones** and delivered materials.
5. **Repeat the starting angles at completion** to show the result.

That guide is written for the U.S. construction market, but the logic applies just as well to a car repair shop, a residential electrical job or a plumbing repair. It is good practice, not legal advice: if a serious dispute comes up, talk to a professional.

## Ground rules: record only with knowledge and permission

A camera in the frame doesn't change something essential about your trade: you work in other people's spaces.

- **Say so before you start:** "I'm going to take photos of the job so we both have a record." Clients who know why you do it usually appreciate it.
- **Ask permission to photograph their home or business,** and avoid capturing people, documents, screens or details that aren't part of the job.
- **Don't record your crew or third parties** without their knowledge.
- **Publishing is a separate decision:** if you want to show the work on social media, get the client's explicit permission and don't show addresses, license plates or personal data.
- **When in doubt, don't capture.**

## A simple routine for each job

1. **On arrival:** photo of the starting condition and of the problem you were told about.
2. **Before covering up:** wide shot and close-up of what will be hidden.
3. **At completion:** repeat the starting angles.
4. **At the end of the day:** tap Import in the OQON app and move the material to your phone.
5. **Name the folder** with client and date, and send the client the photos that concern them.
