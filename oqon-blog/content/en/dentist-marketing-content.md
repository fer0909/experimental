## The problem: your work is visual, but your hands are busy

A dentist has plenty to show: how a treatment is explained, the operatory, the equipment, how a cleaning works, daily care tips. The obstacle is practical. Mid-appointment you're holding instruments with a patient in front of you, and stopping to take a phone photo breaks the rhythm and often means changing gloves.

So many practices end up with a quiet feed or generic stock photos that say nothing about who they are. The content that builds trust is the real kind: how you work and how you treat people.

## First things first: patient consent

In healthcare, a patient's image is sensitive. The rule of this article is a single one: **record only with the patient's knowledge and consent, and publish only what they agreed to.**

U.S. dental marketing guidance agrees. A [legal perspective from MLMIC on patient photos and testimonials](https://www.mlmic.com/blog/dental-patient-reviews-testimonials-legal-perspective/) advises getting written consent before posting patient photos or videos, and not going beyond what that consent covers: if the form says only "photographs," a patient could argue they never agreed to a video. The same source warns that an identifiable photo or video posted publicly without consent is risky. Rules differ by country and state, so check the specifics with your own legal advisor before settling on a release form.

General best practices you can adapt:

1. **A clear written form** stating what is captured (photo, video or both), where it will be posted (Instagram, TikTok, your website) and for how long.
2. **Ask before you start,** not at the end of the visit. The patient should know from the beginning that a camera is in use.
3. **Make it easy to say no** without affecting their care, and to withdraw permission later.
4. **When in doubt, don't post.**
5. **Watch for identifiability:** a before-and-after can identify someone even when the face isn't shown. Review every piece before uploading.
6. **Keep clinical data off screen:** no names, charts or computer screens in the background.

## Content you can make (most of it without showing a patient)

Much of the best dental content doesn't need anyone's face:

- **Your point of view while explaining a procedure:** your hand with the mirror or instrument, no patient on camera, to show the process.
- **A practice tour:** the room, the equipment, instrument sterilization.
- **Daily care tips:** brushing, flossing, what to do after an extraction. You talk to the camera.
- **The team:** who's who, a day at work.
- **Patient FAQs:** answer in 30 seconds what you hear every day.
- **Cases with written authorization:** only when the patient expressly accepted that use.

## A simple weekly workflow

1. **Pick 2 or 3 formats** that don't depend on showing patients (a tour, a care tip, a procedure explanation).
2. **Prepare your authorization form** and keep it at the front desk.
3. **Capture short moments:** 15 to 30 seconds is enough.
4. **Import to your phone at the end of the day** with one tap in the app.
5. **Review every piece** before posting: is any name, data or screen visible? Is there authorization for this use?
6. **Post consistently,** two or three times a week, and answer comments.
