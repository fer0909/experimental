## What are glasses with speakers?

They're regular glasses, frame and lenses, with audio built into the arms. There are no earbuds and no headband: sound comes out of the temple, just above your ear, and travels toward it. You'll also see them called audio glasses, Bluetooth glasses or glasses with built-in headphones.

The pitch is simple. If you already wear glasses all day, why carry a second device that gets lost, needs its own charging and shuts out the world?

## How do they sound? Set your expectations

Open-ear audio is not the same experience as earbuds that seal your ear. Here's what to expect:

- **Clear in quiet places.** At home, at your desk, in a parked car or on a walk through the park, voices on a call and music come through well.
- **No noise isolation.** Your ears stay open, so on a busy street or a crowded train you'll hear the background as usual. That's a safety upside, but these aren't the pick if you want to tune everything out.
- **Someone right next to you may catch a little sound** at high volume. At a moderate level, what you hear stays with you.
- **They aren't a medical device.** If you're looking for help with hearing, talk to an audiologist. Audio glasses are for music and calls.

## Where they earn their place

The value isn't the tech, it's what you can do while your hands are busy:

- **Pick up a call while you're cooking, holding a tool or carrying your kid.** A short tap on the capture button answers, no phone hunting.
- **Listen to a podcast on a walk** and still hear the bike bell, the car horn or the friend calling your name.
- **Take calls while working at home or in the office** and stay present for the person at the next desk.
- **Light workouts like walking or jogging** with music playing and no earbuds falling out. OQON VISION is rated IPX5, so it handles sweat and rain, though it isn't waterproof for submersion.

## What to compare before you buy

A lot of glasses with speakers look alike in a product photo. These questions separate them:

1. **How do you control audio?** Can you play, pause, skip and change volume from the temple without reaching for your phone?
2. **How do you answer a call?** One tap is the goal.
3. **Do they work with your phone?** Look for support for both Android and iPhone.
4. **Can you wear your prescription?** Ask where lenses are fitted and what that costs.
5. **Do they do anything beyond audio?** Some only play sound; others add a camera and a voice assistant.
6. **Do they handle sweat and rain?** Check the water-resistance rating.
7. **Is there a real return window and warranty?** Know what happens if they don't work out.

## Listen smart: volume and breaks

Keeping your ears open doesn't protect them from loud audio. The [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/deafness-and-hearing-loss-safe-listening) recommends keeping volume below 60% of your device's maximum and, if you use an app to measure it, staying below an 80 dB average. It also suggests taking breaks between calls, ideally somewhere quiet.

With open-ear audio, the temptation is to crank it up when it gets noisy. On a loud bus or a busy avenue, pause the music and pick it back up later. And when you're on the road, let your attention go to your surroundings first.
