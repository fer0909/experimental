## Why hands-free recording is harder than it sounds

The moment you want to record is usually when your hands are busy: cooking, fixing a bike, holding a toddler. Before you buy any gear, answer three questions:

1. **Are you staying put or moving?** If the action happens on one table or countertop, a fixed mount is enough. If you walk, climb stairs or switch rooms, you need something that comes with you.
2. **Do you want to be in the shot, or show what you see?** A tripod films you. A chest cam or camera glasses film from your point of view.
3. **How much setup time do you have?** If the moment lasts ten seconds, you won't have time to rig anything.

## Option 1: your phone with a timer or hands-free mode

This is what most people try first, which is why searches like "how to record hands free on iPhone" are so common.

- **The built-in camera timer.** On iPhone, the timer gives you 3 or 10 seconds, but according to [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/access-camera-timer-iphone-11-pro) it isn't available in Video, Slo-mo, Pano or Time-lapse modes. It's a photo tool, not a way to start a video. On Android it depends on the phone maker and software version.
- **Social apps' hands-free modes.** Several social media apps let you record without holding down the button, or add a countdown before recording starts.

**Good for:** a quick clip, a group photo, talking to the camera.
**Not good for:** moving while you record. The phone still needs a place to sit.

## Option 2: a tripod or phone mount

The classic answer: tabletop tripods, floor tripods, clamps for the edge of a table, neck mounts and flexible arms.

**Good for:** talking-head videos, an overhead recipe shot on one counter, a class, an interview.
**Not good for:** following the action. Step out of frame and you've got a great video of a wall.

## Option 3: an action cam on a chest or head strap

**Good for:** mountain biking, a long hike, any sport where you move a lot.
**Not good for:** everyday moments. You carry the camera, the harness and the mounts, strap in before you start, and offload files afterward. Nobody straps on a chest rig to film their kid blowing out birthday candles or to show a client a finished install.

## Three real-life scenarios

- **A recipe.** If you're filming overhead at one counter, a tripod is fine. If you're moving between the fridge, the stove and the oven, camera glasses follow you without losing the shot.
- **Shop or job-site work.** Your hands are on the tool. One tap captures the before and after photos; a double tap records the step-by-step. We cover this in [camera glasses for tradespeople](/en/smart-glasses-for-tradespeople/).
- **A family moment.** The birthday, the first steps, the game. You record without a phone between you and the moment, and with the LED on, everyone knows you're recording.

## Tips for recording hands-free

- **Always record with people's knowledge.** Tell people before you film them, and ask permission in private spaces and at work.
- **Face what you want to show** when recording with glasses: the camera points where your head points. Smooth head movements make for steadier video.
