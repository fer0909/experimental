> Bone conduction headphones send sound as vibrations through the bones of your head; open-ear headphones and audio glasses aim small speakers toward your ear without covering it. Either way your ear canal stays free, so you keep hearing traffic, coworkers and doorbells. The practical difference: bone conduction and open-ear headphones are audio-only accessories, while audio glasses, like OQON VISION (currently sold and shipped in Colombia), put music, calls, a camera and a voice assistant into something you already wear.

## What do they have in common?

All three solve the same problem: listening to music, calls or podcasts without sealing anything inside your ear. Earbuds and over-ear headphones isolate you from noise, which is great on a plane and less great on a sidewalk.

With bone conduction or open-ear audio, your ear stays uncovered. According to [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1402119/bone-conduction-headphones-how-they-work/), bone conduction headphones send sound "via vibrations through bones in your head and jaw" and leave you able to hear your surroundings.

## How does each one work?

**Bone conduction.** The transducers rest against your cheekbones or temples and vibrate. The vibration travels through bone to the inner ear.

**Open-ear audio (headphones or glasses).** Small speakers sit near your ear and aim sound at it, without entering the ear canal. There is no vibration through bone.

Neither protects you from loud volume. The same SlashGear article warns that bone conduction "won't protect you" from hearing loss if you play audio at high volume. Keep the level moderate and take breaks, whatever the technology.

The trade-off to keep in mind: open designs don't isolate. On a loud street or a bus you'll turn the volume up, so ask yourself whether staying aware of your surroundings is what you actually want.

## Which one should you pick?

**Bone conduction or open-ear headphones make sense if:**
- You want an audio-only accessory, mostly for exercise.
- You don't wear glasses.
- You don't mind carrying and charging one more device.

**Audio glasses make sense if:**
- You already wear glasses every day, sunglasses or prescription.
- You want hands-free music and calls without adding another object.
- You'd also use a camera and a voice assistant.
- You'd rather have one thing on your face than two.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is better, bone conduction or open-ear audio?

It depends on what you want. Bone conduction suits an audio-only accessory. Audio glasses suit people who already wear glasses and want music, calls and a voice assistant in one object.

Yes. Your ear stays uncovered. In very loud places you'll need to raise the volume, and at high volume it's wise to take breaks.

### Do bone conduction headphones protect my hearing?

Not by themselves. Even when sound arrives as vibration, excessive volume is still a risk for your ears. Keep the volume moderate with any type of headphones.
