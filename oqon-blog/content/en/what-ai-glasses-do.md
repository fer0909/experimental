## The real question: what are they good for?

When someone searches "what do AI glasses do," they rarely want a technical definition. They want to know whether the glasses will fix something real: stop reaching for the phone to take a photo, ask a quick question without typing, not forget a task. This guide answers that, including what the glasses don't do.

According to [an industry guide](https://evenrealities.com/blog/ai-glasses-guide) (written by a glasses maker, so read it with that in mind), AI glasses are eyeglasses with built-in computing components: microphones pick up your voice, and answers come through small speakers or a display in the lens. The same guide separates them from "basic" smart glasses that only act as Bluetooth headphones or a camera. The difference is the assistant: on top of capturing and playing audio, it understands what you ask.

There are two broad families: glasses that show information on a display inside the lens, and glasses that speak to your ear with no display. OQON VISION belongs to the second family: everything arrives by voice and audio, and your eyes stay free.

## What OQON VISION does, one thing at a time

### 1. Capture without pulling out your phone

One tap on the button takes a photo; a double tap records video, and another tap stops it. It's your point of view, with both hands busy: cooking, fixing something, walking with a child. The camera is 8MP and video is 1080p.

What people often ask: photos and video **don't need internet** and stay on the glasses. When you decide, tap Import in the OQON app and the files move over direct Wi-Fi to your phone. Nothing is uploaded to any cloud.

### 2. Answer your questions in your ear

You speak to the assistant and it answers in your ear, by voice. It's optimized for Spanish and also answers in your device's language. It's handy for quick questions when you'd rather not take your phone out, or can't let go of what's in your hands.

### 3. Describe what's in front of you

Take a photo and the assistant describes what's in it. That helps when you want to know what you're looking at: an object, a package, a scene. It's not a promise that it will always get it right; it's a helper that tells you what it "sees" in the image.

### 4. Record voice notes

Say the idea or the task and it's saved as a voice note. If you work with your hands or think better out loud, it's faster than opening an app and typing.

### 5. Deliver reminders to your ear

This is the line that sums up daily use: no more "I didn't see my phone." The reminder reaches your ear, not a screen you can leave face down.

### 6. Play music and calls

Through open-ear Bluetooth audio: sound reaches your ears without covering them, so you still hear what's around you. The touch area lets you play/pause, skip and slide for volume, and a short tap on the capture button answers a call.

## What you need for the assistant to work

Worth being upfront about, since it's the question that follows the feature list:

- An **OQON account**.
- The **OQON app open in the background** on your phone.
- **Internet on the phone.**

Photos and video, by contrast, work without internet. The glasses work with Android and iPhone.

## What they are not

So you don't buy on a wrong expectation:

- **No display inside the lens.** Everything is voice and audio.
- **Not a hidden camera.** The activity LED lights up whenever the camera is active and cannot be turned off. That's by design: people around you can tell when you're capturing. Record only with the awareness of the people present. Stickers to cover the camera are also included for when you're not using it.
- **Not submersible.** They're IPX5: they resist sweat and rain, not a swimming pool.

## A day with the glasses: what use looks like

**Morning.** You head out with music through open-ear audio. A call comes in and you answer with a short tap, without digging for your phone.

**Work or study.** You dictate a voice note with an idea you don't want to lose. Later, a reminder tells you in your ear that something is pending.

**Afternoon.** You're cooking or in the workshop with busy hands. One tap gives you the photo of the step you wanted to keep; a double tap records the process on video.

**Evening.** You import the photos and videos to your phone whenever you like, from the app, and decide what to do with them.
