## The problem: your best content happens while your hands are busy

Every barber knows when the best part of a cut happens: the fade closing in, the beard line-up, the moment the client checks the mirror. And almost always you're holding clippers or scissors. To film, you have to stop, prop your phone on a makeshift tripod, frame the shot and start over. The result: few videos, or videos where the pause shows.

It matters because video is one of the most natural ways to show your work. In an [article from Modern Barber](https://modernbarber.co.uk/building-a-barbershop-client-base-through-social-media/), several barbers recommend consultation videos because they show how you treat clients, transformation posts because they show your skill, and content that shows the experience a new client can expect. All of it comes out better when filming doesn't interrupt the service.

## What first-person (POV) content means in a barbershop

POV means point of view: the viewer watches the cut through your eyes, as if they were the barber. It works because it shows the real process: the angle of the clippers, your hand, the finishing detail. You don't need production. You need a steady shot from where you're looking.

Video ideas that suit the format:

- **The cut in 15 to 30 seconds:** the central moment of the job, from the barber's angle.
- **The line-up or fade:** the clipper pass, up close.
- **The consultation:** how you listen to what the client wants before starting.
- **Before and after:** one photo when they sit down, one when you finish.
- **A day at the shop:** the atmosphere, the music, the rhythm of the shift.

## Consent: the client first, the camera second

In a barbershop the client is the star, and it's their face and image. The rule is simple: **film only with their knowledge and permission.**

Some practices recommended by marketing guides for salons and barbershops, such as [Socialync's](https://www.socialync.io/blog/hair-salon-marketing-ideas):

1. **Ask before you start,** for example with a line on your intake form: "Okay if we post your before/after online? We will not tag your face or name unless you ask us to."
2. **Keep a simple log** of who said yes, who agreed to photo only, and who said no.
3. **When in doubt, don't post.** That same guide puts it this way: if you're not sure what the client agreed to, default to no.
4. **Offer alternatives:** if a client doesn't want to appear, film only your hands and the clippers, or just the result from behind.
5. **Show them the clip** before posting if they ask.

## A simple weekly routine

1. **Pick 2 or 3 fixed formats** (the 20-second cut, before and after, a day at the shop).
2. **Ask permission when they sit down,** not when you finish.
3. **Film the key moment** with a double tap and stop with another tap.
4. **Import at the end of the shift** with one tap in the OQON app.
5. **Post consistently.** Two videos a week beat ten in one day and nothing after.
6. **Use local hashtags and location** for your neighborhood or city: the Modern Barber article suggests they help more than generic tags.
