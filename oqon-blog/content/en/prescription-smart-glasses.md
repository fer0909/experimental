## The problem: you shouldn't have to choose between seeing well and having the tech

If you have myopia, astigmatism, farsightedness or presbyopia, glasses are already part of your face. Switching to smart glasses without a prescription would mean wearing two pairs, or taking off your usual ones just to snap a photo. That's why people search for "prescription smart glasses": they want one pair that corrects their vision and also does something useful.

The good news is that smart glasses with a camera and audio but **no display inside the lens** work with an ordinary optical lens. The technology (camera, microphone, open-ear speakers) lives in the frame and temples, not in the lens, which makes fitting a prescription far simpler than on glasses with a built-in screen.

## What to ask the optician before lenses are fitted

These questions save surprises, especially with a strong prescription:

- **Will my prescription work in this frame?** The industry acknowledges the limit: according to [an optical lab's guide](https://lensology.co.uk/smart-glasses-prescription-lenses/), most smart eyewear brands struggle to produce lenses for strong or complex prescriptions. It's a commercial guide from another market, so treat it as a reason to ask, not as a rule: check your specific case.
- **Which material and thickness?** Higher prescriptions mean thicker lenses; thinner materials exist.
- **Do I want coatings?** Anti-glare, screen filters or photochromic lenses are choices for you and your optometrist.
- **Am I a multifocal or progressive wearer?** Ask the optician to check pupil height with the frame on.
- **How long will it take?** Ask the optician; it depends on the prescription and the lab.

## Myopia, astigmatism and sports: what changes

**Myopia.** The most common case. With your lenses fitted, you see distance clearly and the camera captures what you see.

**Astigmatism.** Your prescription includes axis and cylinder; the optician cuts them into the lens like in any frame. What matters is a good fit, with lenses centered in front of your pupils.

**Farsightedness and presbyopia.** If you only need glasses for near vision, ask your optometrist whether a full-time lens or a progressive suits you better.

**Sports and active days.** OQON VISION is IPX5: it resists sweat and rain but is not submersible. It's handy for walking, easy jogging or cycling with music through open-ear audio, which lets you hear your surroundings. For contact or high-impact sports, ask your optometrist about safety lenses.
