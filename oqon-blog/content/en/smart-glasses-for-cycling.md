## Three different things people mean by "cycling glasses"

When someone searches for smart glasses for cycling or biking glasses with a camera, they usually want one of three things:

1. **Eye protection** from wind, grit, bugs and glare. That's the job of sport-rated cycling glasses or a motorcycle visor.
2. **A record of the ride**: the climb you've been training for, the Sunday loop, the commute, or what happened at an intersection.
3. **Staying reachable** without letting go of the bars: a call, a playlist, a reminder.

Camera glasses like OQON VISION handle the second and third. The first is still the job of your protective gear.

## What they don't do, and shouldn't be asked to

- **They're not protective eyewear.** OQON VISION are everyday glasses. They don't replace a helmet, sport-rated cycling glasses, motocross goggles or a certified visor. If you ride a motorcycle, try them with your helmet first to make sure they fit comfortably.
- **They're not for fiddling with mid-ride.** Start recording or set a reminder before you roll out or when you're stopped.
- **There's no screen.** Nothing sits in your line of sight to distract you. Everything runs through voice, audio and the camera.
- **They don't record secretly.** An activity LED lights up whenever the camera is active and can't be turned off. When you stop at a café with friends or walk into a place where you don't want to capture anything, the stickers in the box cover the camera so you can wear them as audio glasses.

## What the law says about earphones on a bike

Rules vary by state, so check yours. California is a useful example because its rule is explicit. [California Vehicle Code §27400](https://california.public.law/codes/vehicle_code_section_27400) says a person operating a motor vehicle or bicycle "may not wear a headset covering, earplugs in, or earphones covering, resting on, or inserted in, both ears," with narrow exceptions such as emergency vehicle operators and hearing aids.

Open-ear speakers in a glasses frame don't cover or go into your ears, but the statute doesn't name them either, so don't assume you're in the clear. Ask your local authority if you're unsure. This is general information, not legal advice.

In Colombia, where OQON VISION is sold today, the national traffic code (Law 769 of 2002, article 131, infraction C.38) fines drivers who use a phone while driving unless they use accessories that keep their hands free, according to the text published by [Bogotá's city government](https://www.alcaldiabogota.gov.co/sisjur/normas/Norma1.jsp?i=5557).

## Frequently asked questions

### Are smart glasses good for cycling?

They're good for recording your ride, taking calls hands-free and listening with your ears open. They don't replace a helmet or sport-rated eye protection. Start recording before you set off or when you're stopped.

### Can I use camera glasses on a motorcycle?

You can use them to capture your ride and take hands-free calls, but they're not a substitute for a helmet or a certified visor. Try them with your helmet first to make sure they fit comfortably.

### Is it legal to wear earphones while cycling?

It depends on where you ride. California Vehicle Code §27400 bans earphones covering, resting on or inserted in both ears while operating a bicycle or motor vehicle. Other states have their own rules. Check locally; this isn't legal advice.
