## The short answer: it's about your hands, not the tech

Smart glasses don't replace your phone. What they do is take the phone out of your hand in the moments when pulling it out is a hassle: cooking, holding a tool, carrying a toddler, riding a bike or working with a customer.

So the useful question isn't "are smart glasses good?" It's "how many times a day do I need my phone when my hands are full?" If the answer is "a lot," they'll probably earn their keep. If it's "almost never," keep your money.

## When smart glasses are worth it

These are the situations where people tend to get real value out of them:

- **You work with your hands.** Technicians, mechanics, electricians, barbers, cooks, dentists and inspectors who need to document their work without putting down what they're holding. We cover this in [camera glasses for tradespeople](/en/smart-glasses-for-tradespeople/) and [hands-free inspection documentation](/en/inspection-glasses-hands-free-documentation/).
- **You want to capture moments without missing them.** The birthday, your kid's game, a family recipe, without spending the whole time staring at a screen.
- **You take a lot of calls on the move.** Answer with a tap and talk hands-free, with your ears open so you still hear what's going on around you.
- **You forget things.** A reminder spoken in your ear is harder to miss than an alert on a phone buried in your pocket.
- **You already wear glasses all day.** If you're wearing sunglasses or prescription frames anyway, adding audio, a camera and an assistant doesn't mean carrying another gadget.

## When they're not worth it (yet)

Being honest here helps you make a better call:

- **You want a display in your line of sight.** Many smart glasses, including OQON VISION, have no screen. Everything runs through voice, audio and the camera.
- **You're trying to replace a pro camera.** Glasses are for capturing your point of view hands-free, not for studio-grade production.
- **You want noise isolation.** Open-ear audio leaves your ears free, which is great for awareness but won't block out a subway car or a busy street. More on that in [glasses with speakers](/en/glasses-with-speakers/).
- **Your hands are rarely busy.** Then your phone already does the job.

## 7 questions to answer before you buy

1. **What would you do with them on an ordinary Tuesday?** If you can't name a daily use, wait.
2. **Where do your photos and videos go?** Ask whether they're uploaded to a cloud or stay with you.
3. **Can people around you tell when you're recording?** Look for a visible light that can't be switched off.
4. **Do they work with your prescription?** Ask where prescription lenses are fitted and whether they cost extra.
5. **Do they work with your phone?** Make sure they support both Android and iPhone.
6. **What if you don't like them?** Check the return window and whether the refund is full.
7. **Who helps you if something breaks?** Check where the warranty is honored and what language support is in.

Yes, if you have a clear daily use that needs your hands free: capturing photos and video, taking calls, listening to music or getting reminders. If you'd only use them occasionally, your phone is probably enough.
